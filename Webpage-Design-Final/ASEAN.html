The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) is a regional intergovernmental organization comprising ten member countries in Southeast Asia. It was formed on August 8, 1967, with the aim of promoting economic growth, social progress, and regional stability in Southeast Asia.

Member Countries

  • Brunei Darussalam
  • Cambodia
  • Indonesia
  • Laos
  • Malaysia
  • Myanmar
  • Philippines
  • Singapore
  • Thailand
  • Vietnam
  • Timor Leste

Objectives

The objectives of ASEAN include:

  • Promoting regional peace and stability
  • Enhancing cooperation in economic, social, cultural, and technical fields
  • Facilitating collaboration on issues of common interest
  • Encouraging mutual assistance and cooperation among member countries

Key Achievements

ASEAN has achieved several milestones since its establishment, including:

  • Establishment of the ASEAN Economic Community (AEC)
  • Signing of free trade agreements with various countries and regions
  • Enhancement of regional connectivity through infrastructure development
  • Promotion of cultural exchanges and people-to-people connections
  • Establishment of mechanisms for regional security cooperation
